Cupboard Door Terminal Strip

One row of Phoenix Contact through terminals, linked by plug-in bridges, makes a 3.3 V bus, a GND bus and a terminal for every signal. Only one lead goes to the Pi's 3.3 V pin and one to its GND pin. Top clamps face the Pi, bottom clamps face the driver, switches and sensor.

Parts

  • 17 through terminals, 2.5 mm² size (PT 2.5 push-in or UT 2.5 screw): 5 blue for GND, 4 for 3.3 V (red if you can get them, otherwise grey with a red marker), 8 grey for signals.
  • Plug-in bridges to suit (FBS for PT 2.5 / UT 2.5): one 5-way (T1 to T5), one 4-way (T6 to T9), two 2-way (T10 and T11, T12 and T13). Check the bridge pitch matches your terminal width.
  • 1 end cover for the open side of the last terminal, and 2 end clamps to stop the row sliding.
  • Marker strip numbered 1 to 17.
  • Resistors: 10k (brown black orange) and 4.7k (yellow violet red), quarter watt.
  • Wire: 0.5 mm² with ferrules on the terminal ends. Female Dupont ends for the Pi header and the driver's S, V, G pins.

Fitting the resistors

  • The 10k goes from T9 top to T11 top. T11 is bridged to T10, which carries GPIO16 and EN, so this pulls EN up to 3.3 V.
  • The 4.7k goes from T8 top to T13 top. T13 is bridged to T12, the 1-Wire data line.
  • Resistor legs are thin. Fold each leg back on itself, or crimp a 0.5 mm² ferrule on it, so the clamp grips firmly. Slip sleeving over the legs so they can't touch the GPIO wires they pass over.
  • No resistors yet? Leave T8, T9, T11 and T13 empty. The door still runs for testing. You lose the boot-time driver lock-out and the temperature sensor until they are fitted.

Check before connecting the Pi

Multimeter on resistance, Pi lead and 12 V disconnected.

  1. T6 to T9: all 0 Ω to each other (3.3 V bridge is in).
  2. T1 to T5: all 0 Ω to each other (GND bridge is in).
  3. T6 to T1: open circuit. Anything near 0 Ω is a short between 3.3 V and GND; find it before going on.
  4. T10 to T11 and T12 to T13: 0 Ω.
  5. T10 to T6: about 10 kΩ. T12 to T6: about 4.7 kΩ.
  6. Every signal terminal T14 to T17 to T6 and to T1: open circuit.

Keep in mind

  • The whole 3.3 V bus draws under 5 mA, well within what the Pi's 3.3 V pin can supply.
  • Never put 5 V or 12 V on this strip. Run the 12 V supply and the motor wires straight to the driver board's screw terminals, on a separate part of the rail, in a different wire colour.
  • Use red wire for the single 3.3 V lead (Pi pin 1 to T6) and black or blue for the GND lead (Pi pin 39 to T1).
  • The switches are wired COM to the GND bus and NO to their signal terminal. Pressed reads low.
  • Spare clamps on both buses leave room for more sensors later.

Turn off the Pi and the 12 V supply before changing anything on the strip. A wire slipping from the 3.3 V bus onto a GPIO terminal while powered is harmless; one landing on the 12 V side is not.
